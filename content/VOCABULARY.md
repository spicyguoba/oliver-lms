# Vocabulary library, edition 1

The library contains **2,000 distinct English headwords** and **1,200 distinct simplified Chinese headwords**, with one intended meaning and one example per word. Each set of twenty words has a meaning activity and a sentence-completion activity: 200 English exercise cards and 120 Chinese exercise cards. A round samples five words; the next finished-round replay prefers five other words. Completion indicates a finished round, including a zero score, rather than mastery of all twenty words.

Starter, Growing, and Challenge are broad family practice paths. This is a selection of familiar, everyday, and school vocabulary with extensions, **not a ranked “top 2,000,” validated grade-by-grade sequence, native-Chinese age standard, or HSK certification course**. An adult should select a suitable starting point and discuss unfamiliar ideas. English and Chinese meaning cards use English explanations. Chinese uses Mandarin pinyin and simplified characters; a word may have other meanings or pronunciations beyond the sense on its card. Installed browser voices determine read-aloud availability and quality.

English definitions and complete example sentences were authored for this app. School language covers reading, writing, math, science, geography, history, practical life, and relationships. The final English bank contains no Wordset/WordNet definitions or examples; those were evaluated and rejected in favor of original practice text. Familiar-word prioritization was informed by [Dale–Chall familiar words](https://github.com/words/dale-chall), distributed by Titus Wormer under MIT; its notice is in `licenses/dale-chall-MIT.txt`. The original English practice content is offered under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), attribution “Oliver LMS family practice content, edition 1.”

Chinese vocabulary selection and some glosses/pinyin derive from [Clement Venard's HSK vocabulary](https://github.com/clem109/hsk-vocabulary) (MIT; notice in `licenses/hsk-MIT.txt`) and the [CC-CEDICT project](https://www.mdbg.net/chinese/dictionary?page=cc-cedict), started by Paul Denisowski and maintained by CC-CEDICT contributors. Retain attribution to both. CC-CEDICT-derived vocabulary content is distributed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); adaptations include a selected child-facing sense, simplified glosses, manual pinyin corrections, and original practice examples. These vocabulary data terms do not change the app code's license.

Some Chinese/English example pairs come from the [Tatoeba collection selected by Charles Kelly at ManyThings](https://www.manythings.org/anki/), `cmn-eng.zip`, edition dated 2026-02-13. Those sentence pairs retain **[CC BY 2.0 France](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/)**. Each retained pair includes its original sentence IDs and contributor names in the entry's `attribution` field, also visible in its lesson. Chinese was converted from traditional where applicable to simplified using [OpenCC-JS](https://github.com/nk2028/opencc-js). Examples were screened for unsuitable content, and obvious sense/translation problems were replaced with original examples. A source credit does not guarantee a flawless translation.

Entries marked `Original family practice text` have original practice examples. Entries marked `HSK / CC-CEDICT meaning; original practice sentence` retain the vocabulary provenance but replace the entire example pair. Those new Chinese examples and translations are offered under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution to “Oliver LMS family practice content, edition 1.” Original examples do not retain Tatoeba attribution as if their wording came from Tatoeba. Retained Tatoeba pairs keep their individual original attribution.

Data lives in `content/vocabulary/`: the versioned manifest defines stable twenty-word packs and points to shards of one hundred entries. Existing pack IDs and membership must remain stable after release; add new IDs for future words. Do not regenerate the catalog in a different order over existing learning records. All content is local to the app; no external vocabulary API, advertising, tracking, or generative model is called during learning. Lesson data loads only for the selected pack. Server-owned question snapshots, grading, first-answer records, and stars use the existing native learning flow.
