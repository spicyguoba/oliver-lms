# Map games and people

Content edition 1, collected/reviewed on October 19, 2026. These are short family learning activities, not standardized assessments. New content uses the existing server-graded five-question sessions, first-answer history, stars, completion cards and calendar. There are 35 map exercises and 51 people exercises: fifty ten-person packs and one mixed pack covering 500 distinct people.

## Maps

Seven-continent identification; 94 selected countries in Africa, Asia, Europe, North America, South America and Oceania; world mixes; all 50 U.S. states; five ten-state trails; capital and fact modes. The country selection is an introductory sample, not a complete list of sovereign states. Each round asks five questions; repeated rounds rotate toward least-seen targets. Learners can review cards first, hear a card, search names, zoom and slide the map. Numbered map markers and matching large buttons make small places usable without enlarging the actual land shapes.

We use the seven-continent model with **Australia** as a continent. **Oceania** names the wider region including Australia and Pacific islands. Europe/Asia's boundary is a geographic convention; Russia and Türkiye span both. Regional country grouping is a practice convenience, not a claim that a transcontinental country lies wholly in one continent. Antarctica has no country/capital exercises. Alaska and Hawaii are moved and resized in the U.S. inset map; this is explicitly labeled. Simplified boundaries are learning aids and are not legal definitions or quizzes about disputed areas.

`scripts/build-atlas.mjs` generates static SVG-path JSON under `public/maps/`. Coastlines, continent regions and country geometry come from Natural Earth **5.1.2**, which is [public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/). The [continent regions](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/v5.1.2/geojson/ne_110m_geography_regions_polys.geojson) separate Europe and Asia geographically rather than assigning all Russian land to one continent. Small-scale context is drawn beneath the continent regions. Country geometry uses the 1:50m file. The U.S. map is from [us-atlas 3.0.1](https://github.com/topojson/us-atlas), based on [U.S. Census cartographic boundaries](https://www.census.gov/geographies/mapping-files/time-series/geo/carto-boundary-file.html); its ISC notice is retained in `public/maps/LICENSES.txt`. d3-geo and topojson-client are pinned development tools used to generate paths; the browser does not load a map provider, tile service or third-party script.

Country/state cards link their references. Original state cues were checked against [National Geographic Kids state guides](https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/geography/states), National Park Service topics and established geographic facts. Country references link the named encyclopedia article. Capital teaching includes relevant distinctions: South Africa's executive capital is Pretoria ([government source](https://www.gov.za/south-africas-provinces)); Bolivia's constitutional capital is Sucre; the Netherlands' government works in The Hague; Bern is Switzerland's federal city; Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte is Sri Lanka's parliamentary capital ([municipal source](https://www.kotte.mc.gov.lk/index.php?Itemid=175&id=25&lang=en&option=com_content&view=article)). Indonesia's capital transition is explained on its card and excluded from scored capital questions, since the move to Nusantara is ongoing ([Nusantara Capital Authority](https://ikn.go.id/en/tentang)). Capitals and political geography can change: refresh/review explicitly rather than changing old saved questions.

## People and pictures

The 500-person selection has 90 science discoverers, 60 mathematics/computing contributors, 50 inventors/builders, 80 leaders/changemakers, 50 thinkers/teachers, 60 writers, 70 artists/musicians and 40 explorers/space pioneers. It is an introductory selection, not a ranking or an endorsement of every historical action. Contributions are short original learning cues in `content/people-picks.mjs`. Some achievements were collaborative; shared achievements are described as “helped,” “co-invented,” or similar. Accounts of traditional/uncertain attribution are labeled that way, for example Homer, Laozi and early literary works. General lessons state that historical lives can be complicated.

`scripts/build-people.mjs` resolves each selected encyclopedia title to a distinct Wikidata ID, records the article's revision, and fetches life dates from [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing) (structured data: CC0). Each card links the dated article and life-date record. BCE years are displayed explicitly; ancient/multiple-valued dates are marked “about” and omitted from scored exact-date questions. A missing death date is shown as “born,” rather than claiming that a person is alive. The portrait and achievement questions vary on replay. A picture question also gives an achievement clue. Distractors with identical contributions/lifespans are removed.

The current snapshot includes **425 reusable photos/portraits**. A missing picture has an explicit placeholder; there are no invented or AI-generated likenesses. Pre-photography representations are labeled **Historical portrait or depiction**, and newer material **Photo or portrait**. No claim is made that a much later portrait is a contemporary likeness. Image availability can change at its source.

Only Commons records with public-domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA license metadata and an author/credit are included. Every image retains its file description link, author/credit, license and license URL in `content/people.json`; the interface displays these under **Picture credit**. Thumbnails are resized by Wikimedia; the app performs no other edits. The reuse approach follows [Wikimedia Commons reuse guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia). Missing or unclear reuse metadata is a reason to show a placeholder, not to assume a picture is free.

`/portraits/[id]` caches public thumbnails through the app. It accepts only an ID from the checked snapshot, requests only fixed HTTPS upload/thumb.wikimedia.org URLs, disallows redirects, bounds size/time, accepts raster image content types and returns a self-authored placeholder on failure. No arbitrary URL parameter is accepted. The child's browser does not contact Wikimedia for pictures. Only source/credit links leave the app after being selected. Upstream/cache availability can temporarily affect a picture, while facts and questions remain usable. Source collection caches are ignored `.data/people-sources/`; builds and deployments use the checked snapshot and do not regenerate it or make source API calls.

## Persistence and verification

No schema migration or balance changes are needed for new exercise IDs. Existing sessions retain frozen questions and first answers. A correct map is exposed after grading; private `visualAnswer` is removed from question responses. The fixed public map geometry and learning cards are study material, not secret answer keys. Normal completion pays one star per correct answer, and the unchanged 21-study-star streak rule applies. Existing saved Daily Five plans remain stable; future geography/history rotations can include maps and People.

Tests cover counts and identity uniqueness, all geometry joins, readable targets, valid choices and private keys, rotation, first-answer immutability, idempotent stars, learner isolation, calendar/completion history and portrait URL/type/failure boundaries. Browser QA must additionally check real map/picture rendering, numbered taps, solution feedback, zoom, phone/iPad layouts and resuming a saved round. Physical iPad/Safari remains a separate manual check.
